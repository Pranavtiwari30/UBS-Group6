ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING (AML) POLICY — UBS PAYMENTS DIVISION
Version: 4.2 | Effective: January 2024 | Classification: Internal Use Only

=======================================================================
1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
=======================================================================

This policy establishes the Anti-Money Laundering (AML) framework for
all payment transactions processed by UBS. It applies to all payment
rails including SWIFT, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, and UPI. The policy is
designed to prevent the bank from being used as a conduit for money
laundering, terrorist financing, or other financial crimes.

=======================================================================
2. TRANSACTION MONITORING THRESHOLDS
=======================================================================

2.1 CASH TRANSACTION REPORTING (CTR)
    - All cash transactions above USD 10,000 (or equivalent) must be
      reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).
    - Structured transactions (multiple transactions below the threshold
      designed to avoid reporting) must also be flagged.

2.2 SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTION REPORTING (STR)
    - Transactions that do not align with a customer's known business
      profile must be escalated for review.
    - Unusual velocity: more than 5 transactions within 24 hours to
      the same beneficiary must be reviewed.
    - Transactions with round number amounts (e.g., exactly 10,000,
      50,000, 100,000) require enhanced scrutiny.

2.3 STRUCTURING / SMURFING
    - Multiple transactions of USD 9,000–9,999 within a 48-hour window
      from the same client are indicative of structuring.
    - Such patterns must be flagged and escalated to AML compliance.

=======================================================================
3. CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD)
=======================================================================

3.1 STANDARD CDD
    - All customers must be verified with valid government-issued ID.
    - Business customers must provide proof of incorporation and
      ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) details.

3.2 ENHANCED DUE DILIGENCE (EDD)
    - Required for Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs).
    - Required for customers from high-risk jurisdictions.
    - Required for transactions above USD 10,000.
    - Required when the source of funds cannot be clearly established.

3.3 POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS (PEPs)
    - All PEP transactions require Level 2 compliance officer approval.
    - PEP-related transactions must never be auto-approved.
    - Immediate family members of PEPs are also subject to EDD.

=======================================================================
4. HIGH-RISK TRANSACTION PATTERNS
=======================================================================

4.1 VELOCITY CHECKS
    - Flag if client sends more than 3 large transactions (>USD 5,000)
      in a single business day.
    - Flag if a client's transaction volume increases by >200% compared
      to their 30-day average without a known business reason.

4.2 GEOGRAPHIC RISK
    - Transactions to/from high-risk jurisdictions (see sanctions_rules.txt)
      require additional documentation.
    - Layering: funds passing through 3+ countries before final
      destination is a strong red flag.

4.3 PRODUCT RISK
    - Wire transfers are higher risk than domestic ACH/NEFT.
    - Cash-equivalent instruments (prepaid cards, crypto exchanges)
      are highest risk.

=======================================================================
5. AML HOLD PROCEDURES
=======================================================================

5.1 INITIATING A HOLD
    - Any AML hold must be documented with: transaction ID, hold reason,
      initiating officer, and timestamp.
    - Funds must be quarantined — not returned to sender until reviewed.

5.2 REVIEW TIMELINE
    - Initial review must be completed within 24 business hours.
    - If STR is filed, the transaction remains held until regulatory
      clearance is received.
    - Customer must NOT be informed of a hold if an STR has been filed
      ("tipping off" is a criminal offense in most jurisdictions).

5.3 ESCALATION MATRIX
    - Level 1: AML Analyst — transactions flagged by automated monitoring
    - Level 2: Senior AML Officer — PEPs, sanctions hits, STR filing
    - Level 3: Chief Compliance Officer — >USD 1M, government entities,
      cross-border regulatory requests

=======================================================================
6. RECORD KEEPING
=======================================================================

- All AML-related decisions must be stored for a minimum of 7 years.
- Audit trails must include: detection method, analyst ID, decision,
  and regulatory filing reference (if applicable).
- Records must be available to regulators within 48 hours on request.

=======================================================================
7. PROHIBITED TRANSACTIONS
=======================================================================

The following transaction types are strictly prohibited:
    - Payments to or from entities on the OFAC SDN List
    - Payments to or from UN Security Council sanctioned entities
    - Payments involving shell companies with no verifiable business purpose
    - Transactions designed to obscure the beneficial owner
    - Payments that facilitate bribery or corruption of public officials

=======================================================================
8. TRAINING AND ACCOUNTABILITY
=======================================================================

- All payments staff must complete AML training annually.
- Failure to report suspicious activity is a disciplinary and legal matter.
- Whistleblower protections apply to staff reporting AML concerns.
